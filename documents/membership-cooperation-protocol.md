# Membership Jurisdiction and Agency Cooperation Protocol
**PROPOSED PMAC MEMBERSHIP CONDITION — 5 NOVEMBER 2026 — SUBJECT TO ADOPTION**

## Article 1 — Formal accession
1. A micronation seeking PMAC membership must formally accept this Protocol through an authorised government representative and any internal approvals required by its own constitutional rules.
2. The signed accession must identify the government's designated federal emergency/public-safety agency or, where such an agency does not exist, its equivalent authorised liaison.
3. Applications, including those filed before the adoption of this Protocol, may not be approved until the Registry records fresh express acceptance.

## Article 2 — Internal procedural jurisdiction
1. The member accepts PMAC's authority to administer **institutional compliance proceedings** under the Founding Convention and approved rules.
2. For such proceedings, PMAC may issue:
   - Notices alleging breaches of PMAC membership rules, described as **institutional charges**;
   - Formal summonses requesting attendance or a response in an agreed internal hearing;
   - Procedural directions, document requests and requests to preserve relevant material lawfully;
   - Written findings and internal remedies provided by the adopted Convention.
3. The member undertakes to respond in good faith and facilitate the cooperation of its designated agency or liaison.
4. Membership is **not** general consent to arbitrate every disagreement with a third party; a separate dispute still requires the necessary parties' consent under Article VI.

## Article 3 — Federal emergency or public-safety agency cooperation
1. The applicant government undertakes to secure full **lawful administrative cooperation** from the named agency or liaison, including delivering PMAC notices, coordinating voluntary attendance, preserving material only through lawful and consensual means, and forwarding requests to genuinely competent public authorities where appropriate.
2. The designated agency or liaison shall not impersonate or interfere with recognised emergency services or law enforcement, nor operate beyond the authority it actually possesses.
3. A member shall promptly notify the Registry if its designated liaison or the scope of its lawful authority changes.

## Article 4 — Limits on coercion
1. **This Protocol does not grant PMAC power to arrest, physically detain, search, seize, charge anyone with a criminal offence, or issue binding real-world police orders.**
2. PMAC may request a referral or report to lawfully competent public authorities; such authorities independently decide whether any criminal charge, arrest or other action is justified under applicable law.
3. No member shall treat a PMAC summons or internal institutional charge as a police warrant or criminal indictment.
4. A private micronational undertaking cannot override a person's rights, real-world law, judicial oversight or due process.

## Article 5 — Fair process and institutional remedies
1. A member or individual named in an institutional matter must receive adequate notice, an opportunity to respond, an independent decision-maker and a review route.
2. Following a fair hearing, PMAC may issue a warning, publish a properly authorised institutional finding, suspend membership or recommend removal where the adopted Convention permits.
3. No threat of physical enforcement may be used to secure attendance or obtain evidence.

## Article 6 — Declaration
The applicant government formally accepts institutional cooperation under these limited terms and confirms it has authorised the named federal emergency/public-safety agency or equivalent liaison to facilitate lawful requests.
